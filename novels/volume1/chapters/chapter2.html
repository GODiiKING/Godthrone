Chapter 2

The Scar of Ambition

Zephryn marched forward through the scar of mundus, entering Faelora. Through the collective consciousness, he realized this plane held a stronger frequency, making it easier for the Mere to perform magic, yet the environment felt abrasive. They searched for a place to build their new nation, watching creatures that moved with fluid, alien grace. He found a perfect land to plant the foundation of their expansion, building a castle to serve as a bastion.

As he considered the dangers ahead, he listened to his men discuss the shift in their own awareness. They felt light, as if the physical laws of Mundus no longer constrained their consciousness. Zephryn noticed his son Rivias approaching from behind, his mental frequency jagged with anxiety.

Rivias: Father, I have returned from scouting. There is an uneasiness deeper within. It is another being, but it does not resonate like us.

Zephryn asked about the men who went with him. Rivias said he had left them to watch over the anomaly. Zephryn told Rivias to lead him to it. Rivias apologized, but Zephryn stopped him.

Do not be sorry for what you believe is right, Zephryn said.

They reached the site of the anomaly. The silence was absolute. Zephryn felt it too, a dissonant hum that did not belong to this plane. Then they heard a scream. They found a wounded Mere writhing in agony, his consciousness visibly flickering as it was overwritten by a dissonant frequency. Across the network, other Mere felt a sudden, sharp hiss of static, a psychic tremor that signaled the corruption of their own kind.

Father, please heal my pain, the soldier begged.

Rivias tried to calm him, but Zephryn raised his hand and set the soldier ablaze, ending his life to stop the spread of the dissonance. Rivias stared in shock. Father, I do not understand. If I were in his place, would you do the same?

Before Zephryn could answer, more screams echoed nearby. Rivias rushed ahead. Zephryn followed, his focus sharpened. He found Rivias curled on the ground, his mind fracturing under the pressure of a dreadful, manlike being. The being appeared behind Zephryn, attempting to strike. Zephryn deflected it, his own consciousness rigid and sharp, and severed its arm. The creature fled.

Yet, the scouts were changed, their minds turned into static. Zephryn cut them down, a cold necessity. Rivias fainted from the grief of witnessing the end of his brethren. Zephryn carried his son back to Mundus and informed Toxara of the lurking danger. Before they could speak further, a beam of light engulfed the plane. Aien descended, landing before them.

Do not be afraid, Aien said. At last, we can meet.

Zephryn stood firm. Who are you?

The sphere surrounding Mundus has a low frequency, Aien replied, his voice like the grinding of tectonic plates. I am Aien, the god who sits upon the throne of the Godthrone continent. You invaded my domain. You attacked my child.

Zephryn manifested his blade. What child? Who did I harm?

Aien smirked. Whose arm did you remove today?

Zephryn realized the implications. What is the punishment for an act that cannot be undone?

If you ask, it means you refuse, Aien said, his tone devoid of heat. Then you reject your god. You are now my enemy. Everything you own will be mine.

Zephryn stepped forward. I am the god of my people. I will stand against you.

Aien did not mock him; he simply observed him as one might observe a minor error in a calculation. You will always be beneath me. I do not wish to harm the Godthrone continent. But gather your strongest Mere, for the sphere is open, and dark forces will come to feed on your consciousness. I will claim your soul in mortis, once despair takes your people.

Aien retracted his connection to the plane, his presence snapping away like a severed tether, leaving the Mere in a sudden, freezing silence.

Zephryn stood beneath Solmunda and initiated a deep diagnostic of the collective consciousness. He felt the ripple of static left behind by the scouts, a widening fracture in the network.

He began to calculate the data cost of a war against a being who viewed existence as an asset to be liquidated. He had to prepare his people not just for battle, but for a fundamental rewrite of their reality.
