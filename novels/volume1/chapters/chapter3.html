Chapter 3

The First Awakening

Zephryn returns to his kingdom of Solara and informs his wife Toxara of the being who called himself god upon the throne, Aien, ruler of the Godthrone continent. He tells her that all of Mundus must begin preparing, for the Mere already sense that the Godthrone continent is surrounded by threats beyond their own consciousness. Fear is spreading, but so is awareness. Zephryn says he believes the Mere can survive what is coming. They can grow beyond their current limitations, develop new experiences, and gain greater power than ever before. This moment, though dangerous, is also an opportunity.

Toxara, curious yet cautious, asks what future he envisions for their people now that doubt runs high. The Mere may be swayed to choose sides opposed to their rule. Some may even rebel under the promise of a higher throne. Zephryn agrees the risk is real. Since he chose not to stand beneath Aien, division within Mundus is inevitable. But he refuses to let the Mere remain bound by limits imposed from above. If doubt must come, then it will be shaped into strength rather than submission.

Toxara sighs yet agrees with her husband’s judgment. They ruled with a blade, not an open hand, and the presence of Aien has tipped the balance of power from their grasp. She confesses her fear that when the threat approaches Mundus, their people will spill blood and divide what was once one. She asks Zephryn what he would have of them now.

Zephryn reveals his decision. He will leave the plane of Mundus. He will seek answers and atone for the sins he committed as a benevolent tyrant among the Mere. Aien spoke of the coming Outergods, and Zephryn believes they are not yet powerful enough to defend Mundus from forces beyond their kind. Toxara opposes him. Without his might, how could they survive? Neither she nor their children rival his strength to maintain balance, especially now that they stand exposed against Aien. She reminds him that they expanded beyond Mundus into Faelora, and their son Rivias still bears wounds from the son of Aien.

Zephryn reflects on all he discussed with Aien. If they remain forever defending Mundus and Faelora, they will stagnate while their enemies advance. To only defend is to lose slowly. He answers that he will spread his essence across Mundus, as Aien has done, but not through dominance. He will grant the Mere profound love, something unknown to the Godthrone continent. He will enter a state between life and death, a living corpse sustained by will alone. In doing so, he will grant Mundus true free will, forcing the Mere to unite or divide by their own choice.

Zephryn nods and embraces her. That sin, he says, they must accept as parents. Their many children will choose their own paths. Now the Mere must confront their own doubts and discover what their consciousness can become. He speaks again of a plane called Mortis. In Mortis, he believes answers await about death and about what must be done to secure a better future.

Together, Zephryn and Toxara summon their most loyal among the Mere and announce his atonement. Many of their sons and daughters oppose the decision, fearing greater conflict without Zephryn to hold them accountable. Yet in time even those in opposition accept his choice. Zephryn allocated his power across Mundus then Toxara anchored his consciousness within Mundus. He said farewell to Toxara as his essence spread wide across all of Mundus. Everyone within its plane, the Mere, felt it and knew that their father was no longer physically among them but had become an embodiment within their collective consciousness, empowering them in a way they had never felt before. The greatest gift: free will.

Before Zephryn left Mundus he gave his firstborn sons and daughters their own Legions. His nineteen firstborn became known as the Eminarch, spreading the teachings of their father across Mundus, carving their own paths and forming cultures, councils, and politics, whether noble or flawed.

Rivias, having witnessed how insignificant he and the Mere were compared to what he encountered within Faelora, acted without hesitation. He strengthened his Legions while slowly succumbing to the wound inflicted upon him. The voice of the one who scarred him returned, revealing himself as the son of Aien. The entity did not command him to kneel beneath Aien but surged him to surpass his father in his own way. Thus Rivias followed the being known as Annullon, a Spacegods from Planum Internum. Through this bond, his strength grew, tipping the balance within Mundus. Together, Rivias and Annullon expanded their Legions to shape a new order.

The Mere had once stood united under The Millennium Imperialis, with Toxara inheriting rule. Some siblings remained loyal, but others followed their own ambitions. Rivias founded a new dominion known as The Blackened Dominion. Urged by Annullon, Rivias committed an unforgivable act. He tore a fragment of the heart from his mother Toxara to gain the power to birth more Mere, forever marking himself as fallen. Through that stolen essence and the blood of spacegods, he created stronger offspring. From them he forged an army called The Blackstar Reapers, and he shared the blood of spacegods among loyal Mere, binding them through addiction and ambition.

Meanwhile, those who remained aligned with The Millennium Imperialis such as Zarenor, Seraphon, Aetheris, Calian, Marielas, Marisyl, Liora, Valarae, and Lysara formed a counterforce known as The Luminous Dominion. From Solara, they established themselves as protectors of Mundus. Power fractured the world. Rivias erected a fortress prison within Mundus, a stronghold that drew in criminals and addicts of the blood of spacegods, turning it into a breeding ground of darkness.

As more siblings such as Marikos, Myrranis, Isaryn, Drakara, Mariseth, and Sylitha joined The Blackened Dominion, the influence of The Millennium Imperialis waned. War became inevitable. Calian, whose lands bordered The Blackened Dominion, engaged in relentless conflict. With his Legion known as The Pallid Revenants, he launched a campaign to penetrate Rivias’ stronghold.

Beyond the battlefield, the aura of Zephryn that once encircled Mundus darkened along one edge. That shadow no longer belonged to him but to the rising chaos of The Blackened Dominion. In response, The Luminous Dominion expanded its Legions. Zarenor urged the loyal Mere to take full advantage of the gifts Zephryn had granted. The age of unity had ended. The age of dominion had begun.

The Millennium Imperialis advanced into Planum Internum, seeking the edges of the plane before Rivias could secure absolute control. Dravelyn was the first to set foot there, leading his Legion known as The Hemorrhage of Sight. He was quickly intercepted by Isaryn and her Legion, The Autarchs Stagnation. The conflict divided into structured fronts. Within The Luminous Dominion, defense was governed by The Luminaris. Their offensive counterpart, known as The Umbrum, consisted of Calian, Dravelyn, Marielas, and Lysara. Opposing them, The Blackened Dominion structured The Nocturnis and the assault force known as The Nexum.

Yet the most brutal conflict unfolded within the prison stronghold of Rivias. Calian and his Legion waged relentless war against The Blackstar Reapers. Each descent revealed deeper horrors. Laboratories lined the corridors. Rivias sought to engineer a superior strain of Mere, genetically fortified and infused with the blood of spacegods, vessels capable of serving as a husk for Annullon.

As Calian pressed deeper, resistance intensified. The Blackstar Reapers wielded unfamiliar magic, manifestations of altered consciousness. The prison itself was saturated with the consciousness of Annullon, distorting perception and eroding loyalty. Some among The Pallid Revenants began to crave the substance they were sent to expose. They drank the blood of spacegods in desperation.

Calian uncovered laboratories storing reserves of the blood of spacegods. Even he began to sense Annullon within his own mind. Now he faced a narrowing path. To escape meant survival and warning. To descend further meant possible annihilation. Calian stood at the threshold of decision. Not every Mere could stand among the ranks of the Dominions. War demanded soldiers, but survival demanded far more. Farms had to be tended. Cities maintained. This created a new class among the Mere those who lived ordinary lives beneath the shadow of dominion.

Bandit groups began forming, some turning to theft, others pledging loyalty to The Blackened Dominion. But the greatest destabilizing force was the rise of the blood of spacegods. Smugglers distributed it across hidden networks. Addiction followed. Those who used the blood too often began to change. Flesh twisted. Entire settlements now feared those who had become slaves to the substance.

Criminals and addicts were taken as prisoners and cast into the stronghold of Rivias. To some it was a prison. To others it was a sanctuary. For the prison stronghold was not merely stone; it had become an extension of Annullon. Stories spread of Calian and his men, trapped in a war without end. In response, The Luminaris issued strict decrees forbidding any Mere from seeking the stronghold.

Yet the power of the blood came with a cost. Without restraint, it transformed users into violent aberrations. From these power hungry ambitions came the rise of a group known as The Nachash. They are seen as a direct embodiment of evil. Their ruler is Moloch, who strikes enemies and allies alike. Alongside him walks Mestema, who spreads theodicy, while Belial spreads worthlessness and wickedness. Then there is Baalzebul, who brings death in his wake.

Their presence is felt across Mundus, Faelora, and Planum Internum. The Nachash do not ally with either Dominion. They remain between them, maintaining a dark balance. Their origin comes from the experiments Rivias and Annullon conducted within the stronghold to create a body capable of walking within Mundus. Moloch, Mestema, Belial, and Baalzebul are four children stronger than even The Blackstar Reapers.

Moloch leads them, devouring the fallen as sacrifices. Because of this, The Luminous Dominion views him as a dreadful king. Belial walks beside him, breaking the will of the Mere from within and spreading lies about Zephryn. To him, destruction is liberation. Beside them stands Mestema, who watches the trials of the Mere and laughs at their sacrifices. He believes that one day the misfortune of the Outer gods will reach Mundus. The final member is Baalzebul, who inspires envy and murder, waiting for the moment when corruption brings about the downfall of the believers.

While everyone was distracted, the prison stronghold expanded further into Mundus. It appears as though Noctmunda has been permanently placed above it. The leaders of The Luminaris now question the fate of Calian. At the center of this mystery stands a massive tower formed through the power of Annullon. It acts as an anchor for his influence. Its presence divides the Mere even further.

At the peak of the tower stands Rivias. Looking upward he sees what he understands to be space, and notices something otherworldly watching him. He realizes the plane beyond is known as Umbra. Rivias announces that the place where the stronghold stands will now be known as Babel. The name spreads like wildfire. Lysara becomes furious when the council forbids her from marching to Babelon to rescue Calian. Zarenor believes patience is necessary, fearing that rushing in will only cause them to share the same fate.
